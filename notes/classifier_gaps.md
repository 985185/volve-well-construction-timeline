# Classifier v3 suspected gaps (for the future journal version; v3 is frozen in this paper)

Do not act on these in the IPTC-25544-MS rerun.

1. **Non-ASCII characters not normalised.** `normalise_text` replaces only ½, ¼, ¾, ⅛. The narratives (sum24Hr plus forecast24hr) also contain ³ (31), Ø (13), æ (9), ¿ (3), Æ (2), Å (1). `³` appears in volume units; `Ø` is used as a diameter or "at" symbol (e.g. `Ø 3100M`, `TD Ø 3220 M`) and may hide depth or hole-size context; `¿` (3) looks like a corrupted character in the source. Source: Stage 3 Unicode survey, `outputs/stage3_unicode_survey.csv`.
2. **The "8½" pattern in "Reservoir drilling" (`8½.{0,5}(?:hole|section)`) can never match after normalisation**, because ½ is replaced by " 1/2" first. **Confirmed in Stage 4:** the pattern matches 0 of 1,759 normalised narratives, so it is dead code. It does no harm because the same sizes are caught by `8.{0,3}1.2.{0,5}(?:hole|section|BHA)` after normalisation (19 narratives contain a literal `8½` followed by hole/section; their phase assignments are unchanged because Table 2 reproduces exactly).

3. **Future (journal version, not part of this rerun):** Independent kick-off check from directional survey files, if present in the Volve archive.

Added after the external review of November 2026 (single-report blind agreement 30 of 52, outputs/classified_audit_agreement.csv). The items below are for the journal version only; the classifier is not changed in this paper.

4. **Context-based audit (deferred).** A second audit that shows an engineer the five reports around each sampled day (D-2 to D+2) was designed and cancelled for this paper (decision 83). The single-report audit leaves open how often the neighbouring reports decide the phase. The journal version should run it, with a new sample of classified reports (Workover has only 2 unused reports after the 52 of the single-report audit, so the sample cannot be 4 per phase for every phase), the neighbouring reports shown, and a record of when the engineer needed them.
5. **Sequence-aware classification.** The classifier reads one report at a time. An engineer assigns a day using the neighbouring reports and the state of the campaign (which section is being drilled, whether casing is run, whether a sidetrack, a plug and abandonment or a completion campaign is under way). Candidate design: carry the campaign state forward and use it to resolve days that name no size, mix operations, or reach a phase through an incidental word.
6. **Workover rule and whipstock or window milling.** All four Workover reports in the single-report audit were whipstock or window milling (15/9-19 A, 15/9-F-9), which the author labelled Intermediate drilling. The Workover patterns (sidetrack, whipstock) do not distinguish a sidetrack window in a drilling campaign from intervention work. Candidate rule: give whipstock, window milling and sidetrack-window language its own phase or route it by context.
7. **Incidental-keyword triggers.** Eight of the 22 disagreements are days on which a word names an operation that is not the main one (cement head, WOC, a liner pressure test, a laid-down BHA). The priority order takes the first pattern that matches anywhere in the text. Candidate fix: score the whole narrative, weight the operations named first, or require the keyword in an operation clause.
8. **Phases the audit suggests are missing.** From the unexplained and context-dependent disagreements: abandonment and riser or wellhead retrieval days (P&A campaign), well hand-over and XMT testing (Workover or Completion), and BOP running after waiting on weather (Rig move/Setup). The single-report audit gave no "Other" labels, so this list is indicative and is to be confirmed by the author before any phase is added.
9. **Casing sizes in the size-based phases.** Two disagreements (14 in. and 9 5/8 x 10 3/4 in. casing runs) come from the size-based phase names: the classifier places 13 3/8, 10 3/4 and 14 in. casing in Intermediate casing, and the author labelled the day Liner/Casing run. The scheme is a phase-naming choice and may be revisited.
