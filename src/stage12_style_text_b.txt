The population of ten WITSML-derived columns was surveyed across all 1,759 DDRs (Table 3).
@@ 4 Data Quality | The structured kick-off field is populated in only 14.10%
The structured kick-off field is populated in only 14.10% of rows (248 of 1,759), so sidetrack kick-off depths had to be recovered from the narratives and from depth sequences (Section 5.6).
@@ 4 Data Quality | A standard null-or-blank check reports
A standard null-or-blank check reports 100% population for sum24Hr, but 73 records hold the literal string "None". The string sits in the source XML, in 73 of the 1,759 files, and is a placeholder of the archive. A null test cannot detect it, and the genuine narrative population is 95.85% (1,686 of 1,759). The same placeholder fills 103 forecast24hr values, which leaves a genuine forecast population of 94.14% (1,656 of 1,759). The archive holds 515 placeholder values across all extracted fields, made up of 331 depth sentinels, 7 zero depths, 176 literal "None" strings (73 narratives and 103 forecasts) and one date sentinel.
@@ 4 Data Quality | A character-level survey of sum24Hr found
A character-level survey of sum24Hr found two non-ASCII characters that matter for classification, the one-half sign (½) in 61 DDRs (82 occurrences) and the one-quarter sign (¼) in 10 DDRs (14 occurrences). Both are normalised before pattern matching. The raw files declare the ISO-8859-1 character encoding, and reading them with another encoding turns ½ into Â½ (for example 'due to double 3 Â½" DP' for 'due to double 3 ½" DP'). The encoding must therefore be set explicitly. One further anomaly is a single record for 15/9-19 A dated 1 January 1980 (filename 15_9_19_A_1980_01_01.xml, narrative "None", no measured depth), 17.6 years before the first genuine report of the wellbore on 25 July 1997. It is a date sentinel.
@@ 5.1 Classification Rate | Applying the classifier to all 1,759 DDRs
The classifier assigns a phase to 1,303 of the 1,759 DDRs (74.1%) and leaves 456 unclassified (25.9%) (Table 4). The classifier was also checked against an independent earlier extraction of the same archive by the author, with identical results for all 1,759 reports. Fig. 1 shows the classified phases on the depth-versus-time curves of four representative wellbores.
@@ 5.2 Stratified Audit and Attainable Classification Rate | Of the 50 sampled reports, 26 (52%)
A complete pattern set would classify about 85.4% of the 1,759 reports (95% confidence interval 82.5 to 88.3%). Of the 50 sampled reports, 26 (52%) were labelled construction and 24 (48%) non-construction. The construction group (39 reports) split 23 construction to 16 non-construction, and the production group (11 reports) split 3 to 8. Of the 26 construction reports that the classifier missed, 9 reported genuine drilling without a hole size, 10 described other operations with no corresponding pattern, such as hydraulic fracturing or fishing, and 7 used non-standard terms, such as "CMT" for cement. Scaled to the 383-report frame, the 26 correspond to about 199 reports. The arithmetic, including the stratum-weighted check, is in Appendix B.
@@ 5.2 Stratified Audit and Attainable Classification Rate | The 73 DDRs whose narrative is the literal string
No text method can classify the 73 DDRs whose narrative is the literal string "None". They are excluded from the attainable rate, and even a perfect pattern set leaves at least 4.2% of DDRs (73/1,759) unclassified.
@@ 5.3 Timeline Dataset | The timeline dataset contains one row per DDR
The timeline dataset holds 1,421 rows, one for each DDR with a genuine md_m value. Of the 1,759 DDRs, 331 carry the sentinel and 7 the zero value. Each row gives the wellbore identifier, the report date, the days from the wellbore's first report with a recorded depth, the measured depth, the assigned phase and the source filename. Days are counted from the first row with a usable depth in each wellbore. Fig. 2 plots report number against measured depth for all 26 wellbores. The report number counts every DDR of a wellbore from its first report (1980 sentinel excluded), with or without a recorded depth, and so matches the reports to TD in Table 8. Only reports with a valid depth are plotted.
@@ 5.4 Total Depth Validation | Of 26 wellbores, 24 have a defensible NOD counterpart
Of the 26 wellbores, 24 have a defensible NOD counterpart, and 22 of these agree exactly on total depth. 15/9-F-15 differs by 15 m (0.4%) and 15/9-19 S by 227 m. Among the 19 F-series wellbores matched by literal name, 18 (94.7%) agree exactly, and 15/9-F-15 is again the exception. 15/9-F-7 and 15/9-F-9 share a total depth of 1,085 m, and NOD independently records 1,085 m for each, which rules out a duplication in the extraction.
@@ 5.4 Total Depth Validation | The two unmatched wellbores and the three depth-based matches
One explanation covers the two unmatched wellbores and the three depth-based matches. Each WITSML technical sidetrack (19 BT2, 19 ST2, F-11 T2) has no separate NOD entry, and NOD records the final total depth of the sidetrack against the parent designation (19 B, 19 SR, F-11). The WITSML parent records that end short of that depth, 15/9-19 B at 3,272 m and 15/9-F-11 at 347 m, are the legs abandoned when the technical sidetrack was drilled, and they have no NOD counterpart. The stuck-pipe event of 15/9-19 B is documented in the narrative (Section 3.5), and the designation itself is the evidence for 15/9-F-11. The operator's WITSML system tracks each drilled leg while the national register records the wellbore. The two registers therefore differ by construction, and neither is in error.
@@ 5.4 Total Depth Validation | 15/9-19 S was an original exploration wellbore
The WITSML series of 15/9-19 S ends at 3,353 m, 227 m short of NOD's structured total depth (3,580.0 m). The well was an original exploration wellbore targeting the Theta Vest structure north of Sleipner East and planned as deviated, consistent with its "S" suffix. NOD's wellbore history records it as temporarily suspended at 3,569.0 m (2,394.0 m TVD RKB), immediately above the Heimdal sands (NOD 2026d), which is 216 m deeper than the last WITSML depth. The 11.0 m difference between the two NOD values lies within the register itself, and Table 5 uses the structured field for this wellbore as for every other. The archive holds no reports for the final drilling and suspension of 15/9-19 S, and the 227 m gap reflects those missing records. The reports it does hold are incomplete throughout, 39 reports over 91 days (Table 8), and the records do not show why.
@@ 5.5 Spud Date Validation | Spud date was compared across three sources
Spud date was compared across three sources for every wellbore, the WITSML spud_date field, free-text spud language in the earliest DDRs of the wellbore and the NOD entry date. The offset is the WITSML spud date minus the NOD pre-drill entry date where NOD records one, because it marks the start of top-hole drilling, and otherwise the NOD main entry date. Table 6 summarises the outcome and Fig. 4 plots the offset by category. For the four wellbores whose archive contains the NOD pre-drill entry (15/9-F-4, F-5, F-14 and F-15), the first report falls on that date, or one day later for F-4, which supports the pre-drill date as the spud reference. The categories were first assigned by inspection of the three sources for each wellbore, and the rules in the Rule column of Table 6 were formalised afterwards to describe those assignments. Applied mechanically, the rules reproduce 25 of the 26 assignments. The exception is 15/9-F-11, which was assigned Exact by inspection and is unresolved under the rule. NOD records a pre-drill campaign (6 to 17 April 2012) that is absent from the DDR archive, and the archive's 2013 reports describe a fresh spud from seabed, including the 30-inch conductor. The records do not show how the two campaigns relate.
@@ 5.5 Spud Date Validation | In the four error cases
In the four error cases the structured spud_date differs from the first report and its narrative by 91 to 340 days, while the first report and the NOD date agree with each other. 15/9-F-12 is the clearest case. Its first report (14 June 2007) describes making up the 36 in. hole opener and agrees with the NOD entry date, and only the structured field (15 March 2007) disagrees. Two sidetracks, 15/9-F-11 A and 15/9-F-11 T2, carry a spud_date identical to that of their parent, which is almost certainly an inherited field value. NOD does not register 15/9-F-11 T2 separately, and Fig. 4 excludes it for that reason.
@@ 5.5 Spud Date Validation | 15/9-F-1 required particular care
The spud date of 15/9-F-1 needed particular care. Its WITSML spud_date (28 November 2007) falls four days before its first DDR (2 December 2007), which narrates drilling 36 in. hole from the seabed, an ordinary gap between spud and first report. The 2007 reports cover the top hole only (four reports, maximum depth 226 m). The well was drilled to its total depth of 3,632 m in the 2013 campaign, whose first report (22 July 2013) is one day before the NOD entry date (23 July 2013). NOD records no pre-drill date for the 2007 work, unlike F-4, F-5, F-14 and F-15. The NOD date therefore marks the later campaign, and the case is classified as "NOD date marks later campaign". The WITSML value is consistent with the chronology of the wellbore.
@@ 5.6 Sidetrack Kick-Off Depth Recovery | Because the structured kick-off field is sparse
Kick-off depth was recovered from the DDRs because the structured kick-off field is sparse. Kick-off here means the depth at which a sidetrack departs its parent wellbore, not the depth at which an original wellbore begins to build angle; the structured kick-off field of 15/9-19 S (562 m in all its reports) is consistent with the latter and is not used. One rule was applied to all sidetracks, and the depth was read from the last report of the parent and the first report of the sidetrack. The kick-off is the depth at which the narrative first reports new formation or departure from the parent hole. Where the narrative states none, it is the depth at which the kick-off or window was started, and for casing exits by a milled window it is the top of the window. Where the narrative is empty, it is the measured depth at the parent-to-child transition.
@@ 5.6 Sidetrack Kick-Off Depth Recovery | All 15 sidetracks by designation
A kick-off depth was recovered for all 15 sidetracks (Table 7). The depth is written in the narrative for 11 of them. For 3 (15/9-F-1 A, F-1 B and F-15 D) the narrative names the kick-off event but gives no depth, and the depth is taken from the measured depth of the same report. For 15/9-19 ST2 both reports at the transition are empty. The depth is the measured depth there, which makes it the weakest case. The last report of F-1 B (22 February 2014) reads "Commenced time drilling to1364 m. 50% formation obtained at 1364 m.", and the first report of F-1 C drills 17½ in. hole from that depth, so the well was sidetracked at 1,364 m. The narratives report 3,059 m on 13 March 2014, but that depth marks the start of the 8½ in. section after casing was run. The sidetrack 15/9-19 A kicked off at the top of a milled window at 2,178 m (window milled from 2,178 to 2,183 m), a depth that NOD also records.
@@ 5.6 Sidetrack Kick-Off Depth Recovery | NOD's wlbKickOffPoint is available for 12 of the 15
NOD's wlbKickOffPoint agrees with the recovered depth to within 10 m for 10 of the 12 sidetracks for which it is available. It equals the recovered depth for 6 (15/9-19 A, F-1 A, F-1 C, F-15 A, F-15 B and F-15 D), differs by 10 m or less for 4 (15/9-F-9 A, F-11 A, F-11 B and F-15 C) and by more for 2 (15/9-19 B, 31 m; 15/9-F-1 B, 13 m). NOD has no kick-off value for the other 3 (15/9-19 BT2, 19 ST2 and F-11 T2). The least certain depths are 15/9-19 ST2, which rests on the measured depth alone, and 15/9-F-11 B, where cement and new formation were drilled from 2,531 to 2,589 m and the end of the interval is taken as the departure.
@@ 5.7 Duration to Total Depth | Table 8 gives, for each wellbore
Table 8 gives the duration of each wellbore from its first report (the 1980 date sentinel excluded) to the date total depth was first reached, which is the maximum valid depth of Table 5. It lists the elapsed calendar days, the number of reports in that span (inclusive), the drilled length and the metres per report-day. The drilled length is total depth minus the kick-off depth of Table 7 for the 15 sidetracks and total depth for the other 11, and the rate divides it by the number of reports. Elapsed days and reports agree where a wellbore was reported daily and differ where the archive has gaps. Seven of the 26 wellbores, whose elapsed days exceed twice their reports, are flagged (15/9-19 S, F-1, F-4, F-5, F-9, F-14 and F-15). F-1 reaches its total depth of 3,632 m on 17 August 2013, 2,085 days after its first report on 2 December 2007, in 31 reports, four of them from 2007. The totals of 15/9-19 B (3,272 m) and 15/9-F-11 (347 m) are the depths of an abandoned parent leg or stub (Section 5.4) and make their durations indicative only. Drilled lengths and rates for sidetracks depend on the kick-off depths of Section 5.6. The rates are per reported day, and for F-1 they mix two campaigns. Per-phase report counts for each wellbore and well family are published with the duration table.
