@@ 1 Introduction | Daily Drilling Reports are the primary operational record
Daily Drilling Reports (DDRs) are the primary operational record of a well construction campaign. Each report logs depth progress, casing and cementing operations, formation encounters and non-productive time. In the WITSML (Wellsite Information Transfer Standard Markup Language) exchange format (Energistics n.d.), a DDR holds structured fields such as measured depth, spud date and casing depths, together with a free-text narrative of the preceding 24 hours of operations. Turning the archive of a field into a construction timeline means assigning every report to a construction phase, and the inconsistent terminology, abbreviations and narrative text that accumulate over a field's multi-decade life make that difficult. This paper reconstructs the timeline of the Equinor Volve field from 1,759 WITSML DDRs covering 26 wellbores. A deterministic, priority-ordered rule-based classifier reads the narrative of every report. A stratified sample of the unclassified remainder shows what the classifier misses, and total depth and spud date are validated for every wellbore against an independent national register, the NOD FactPages (NOD 2026a). The data-quality problems that any other user of the archive will meet are documented along the way. A reader with this text, the openly available Volve dataset (Equinor 2018) and the accompanying repository should be able to reproduce every reported figure.
@@ 2 Background and Related Work | Several studies have extracted structured information
Several studies have extracted structured information from the free text of drilling reports. Sidahmed et al. (2015) built a dictionary of rig terms and acronyms with drilling experts and tracked weighted term frequencies over time in the reports of three wells to flag recurring operational concerns. Priyadarshy et al. (2017) presented a framework for predicting the causes of non-productive time from unstructured reports. Hoffimann et al. (2018) applied deep natural language processing to 9,670 daily reports from a field of 303 wells and classified each sentence as an event, a symptom or an action. They noted that technical symbols, abbreviations, misspellings and incomplete sentences make the text difficult to process. Zhang et al. (2022) built a rule-based detector for five unscheduled events (stuck pipe, lost circulation, water influx, gas influx and H2S) in about 128,000 free-text remarks from daily mud reports. The detector was precise but missed events, and its accuracy fell to 65.18% on a larger test set. A hybrid that used its output to train a weakly supervised deep learning model improved detection by more than 20 percentage points.
@@ 2 Background and Related Work | Karnot et al. (2025) combine change-point detection
Karnot et al. (2025) combine change-point detection, semi-supervised natural language processing and sequence mining for root-cause analysis of non-productive time and apply the combination to 701 DDRs from 10 wells. Their Dataset section states that "all analyses used synthetic datasets constructed to emulate typical drilling records". Their sequence-mining section, which they describe as a proof-of-concept analysis, states that "rigorous validation and quantitative performance assessment are left for future work". Their abstract and conclusions nevertheless report an average 18.5% reduction in non-productive time in field applications.
@@ 2 Background and Related Work | The Volve dataset itself is an established benchmark
The Volve dataset is an established benchmark for drilling analytics. Tunkiel et al. (2021) derived a reference dataset for rate-of-penetration benchmarking from its drilling data. Commercial analyses of Volve DDRs for subsets of wells have been described informally. To the author's knowledge, no validated field-wide construction timeline from the Volve DDR narratives has been published.
@@ 2 Background and Related Work | The common limitation across the published DDR work
Published DDR work relies on proprietary or synthetic data, which prevents others from reproducing or extending its results. The present work (1) uses a deterministic, auditable classifier that trades model sophistication for transparency of every decision, (2) works exclusively with a real, openly licensed archive, and (3) pairs classification with a stratified audit of the unclassified residual, using a documented sampling frame and a Wilson-score interval (Wilson 1927), and with validation of total depth and spud date against a national register for the complete wellbore population. The audit in this paper measures the weakness that Zhang et al. (2022) report, the events a rule-based classifier misses, directly on the unclassified reports.
@@ 3.1 Data Source and Extraction | The DDR archive was extracted from the Volve dataset's
The 1,759 WITSML 1.4.0.0 XML files of the Volve dataset (21,315,879 bytes, each recorded with a SHA-256 hash) were extracted into a single table with one row per DDR and no duplicate wellbore and date pairs. The rows cover the exploration well 15/9-19 and the platform wells 15/9-F-1, F-4, F-5, F-7, F-9, F-10, F-11, F-12, F-14 and F-15, which make up 26 wellbores in 11 well families. The columns include the source XML filename, the well and wellbore identifiers, the report date, the spud date (WITSML dTimSpud), measured depth (md_m), kick-off depth (md_kickoff_m), last casing depth (md_csg_last_m), operational parameters and the free-text 24-hour narrative (sum24Hr). Every field was kept as a raw string to keep placeholder values visible, and the published code regenerates the table from the raw files.
@@ 3.1 Data Source and Extraction | The md_m field is stored as a string and contains two placeholder
The md_m field is stored as a string and contains two placeholders, the literal '-999.99' (a missing-value sentinel) and the literal '0'. Both were excluded and the remaining values cast to a numeric type before any depth arithmetic.

@@ 3.2 Wellbore Designation | Norwegian wellbore names follow guidelines
Norwegian wellbore names follow the designation guidelines of the national regulator (NOD 2025). The suffix "S" marks an exploration wellbore planned to be deviated, letters A, B and so on mark sidetracks, and "R" marks a re-entered exploration wellbore. A technical sidetrack, drilled to bypass a mechanical problem such as stuck pipe, is designated T2 for the first and T3 for the next. The Volve WITSML archive uses the technical-sidetrack designations 15/9-19 BT2, 15/9-19 ST2 and 15/9-F-11 T2, and none of them appears as a separate entry in the NOD register for the field. This one convention explains every naming mismatch between the two registers (Section 5.4).
@@ 3.3 Phase Classification | Each DDR's sum24Hr narrative is classified into one of 13 phases
The classifier assigns the sum24Hr narrative of each DDR to one of 13 phases: Spud/Conductor, Surface hole and casing, Intermediate drilling, Intermediate casing, 12¼ in. and smaller hole, Liner/Casing run, Cementing, Completion, P&A, Logging/Testing, Workover, Waiting/Downtime and Rig move/Setup. Each phase has a fixed priority from 1 (highest) to 13 (lowest), in the order listed, and a set of case-insensitive regular expressions. A DDR takes the highest-priority phase with a matching pattern, and a narrative that matches no pattern stays Unclassified. Fraction characters such as ½ and ¼, routine in the mixed units of drilling reports, are stored as single special characters that a pattern written as "1/2" does not match. They are converted to that form before matching. The phase names refer to the hole and casing sizes that each pattern group matches. On Volve platform wells the 12¼ in. hole is usually the section above the reservoir. Every regular expression is listed in the repository file src/classifier_v3.py.

@@ 3.4 Stratified Audit of the Unclassified Residual | The audit estimates how many unclassified DDRs
The audit estimates how many unclassified DDRs describe construction operations that the pattern set failed to catch. A leak check first reapplied the complete pattern set to every unclassified record and found no record that should have matched. Every unclassified record was then assigned to one of eleven descriptive categories, for example BOP and riser handling, generic trips, production monitoring and empty records, using patterns built from inspection of the unclassified population. BOP and marine riser handling is the largest category (147 of 456 records, 32.2%). The 73 empty records have a sum24Hr field that holds the literal string "None" (Section 4). No text method can classify them, and they were excluded from the sampling frame, which left 383 records.
@@ 3.4 Stratified Audit of the Unclassified Residual | A stratified sample of 50 records was drawn from these 383
A stratified sample of 50 records was drawn from the 383. The unclassified reports were split into two strata, an early construction group (reports within 730 days of the wellbore's first report, 296 records) and a later production group (reports more than 730 days after it, 87 records), and a random sample was drawn from each in proportion to its size, 39 records from the construction group and 11 from the production group. The random draw is recorded in the repository and allows the same sample to be drawn again. The author read each sampled report and labelled it construction if it described work that builds the well (drilling ahead, coring, running or cementing casing, setting plugs, fishing or completion). Reports that described only BOP or riser handling, tripping, waiting or production were labelled non-construction unless they also named a hole or casing size.
@@ 3.5 External Validation Against NOD FactPages | Total depth and spud date were compared with the NOD FactPages
Total depth and spud date were compared with the NOD FactPages (NOD 2026a). The Table View export was filtered to the Volve wellbores and matched by exact name wherever possible. Three WITSML wellbores, all technical sidetracks, were matched by depth (15/9-19 BT2 to NOD 15/9-19 B, both 4,250 m; 15/9-19 ST2 to NOD 15/9-19 SR, both 4,641 m; and 15/9-F-11 T2 to NOD 15/9-F-11, 4,562.1 m and 4,562.0 m). The export was retrieved on 24 October 2026. Its rows for the Volve wellbores match an export of 8 September 2026 except for the register's own synchronisation date. All comparisons are against the current register as accessed in October 2026.
@@ 3.5 External Validation Against NOD FactPages | For the 15/9-19 series, the DDR narratives show
In the 15/9-19 series the DDR narratives describe the events behind the designations, and the NOD wellbore histories (NOD 2026b, 2026c, 2026d) are consistent with them. The drillstring in 15/9-19 B became stuck at 3,272 m. The string was backed off and cut, a sidetrack plug was set, and new hole was kicked off from 2,911 m as 15/9-19 BT2, whose first report reads "REPORTING TRANSFERRED FROM 15/9-19B". Well 15/9-19 S followed the same sequence, with stuck pipe at approximately 2,741 m, a backed-off and cut string, sidetrack language in the narrative ("MU BHA FOR SIDE TRACK") and new hole from approximately 1,536 m as 15/9-19 ST2. In both cases NOD records the final total depth against the parent designation.
